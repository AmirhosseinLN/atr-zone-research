Short answer. Only XAUUSD shows an edge that holds in both periods and across almost every parameter setting. EURUSD, DAX is worth watching. Across all markets together the strategy lost money in the discovery period and only barely made money afterwards (p ≈ 0.10, not significant) — so it is not a general system; it is a gold system.

One shared rule set was chosen on pooled discovery data only. "Settings OK in both" counts how many of 32 parameter sets had positive expectancy in discovery and validation — a market that only wins with one lucky setting is not reliable.

Pooled over all markets and all settings, the volatility-regime filter did not help — it was slightly worse in both periods. It is not a universal edge; at best it is specific to gold.

Why not just pick each market's best setting?

Choosing the best of 32 settings per market on the discovery period gives an average expectancy of +0.260R there — which falls to +0.100R in validation across 33 markets. That shrinkage is selection luck, which is why the table uses one universal setting plus the "settings OK in both" breadth count.

About the "Regime luck" markets (COPPER, EURJPY, XAGUSD, US30, GBPJPY, LINKUSD, BRENT, BTCUSD, USDJPY, XRPUSD, AUDJPY, DOGEUSD, SOLUSD)

These (including the JPY pairs) had flat or negative results before 2025-08-01 and strong results after it. That pattern usually means the recent market happened to suit the strategy (clean trends) rather than a lasting edge. Do not trade them on this evidence.

Caveats

  • Free hourly data covers only ~2–3 years, so each market has roughly 20–60 trades per period. Differences between nearby numbers are noise.
  • 33 markets × 32 settings is a lot of looks; a few will look good by chance. That is why consistency across settings and periods, not a single big number, decides the verdict.
  • NIKKEI has only 2 hourly bars per day on Yahoo (cash-index hours), so it could not be tested; DAX is similarly sparse, so its verdict is tentative.
  • Costs are round-trip spread+slippage in basis points of price by asset class (FX 1.5–2.5, metals 2, energy 5, indices 1.5, crypto 6–12). Real broker costs may differ.
